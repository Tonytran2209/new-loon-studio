Designing useful AI-assisted experiences

Design & AI · Studio note

The most useful AI-assisted experience starts with a clear user need. Technology becomes valuable when it reduces effort, explains choices or helps someone complete a task.

Start with a task

Describe what a person is trying to do before choosing a model, tool or interface. A focused task makes it easier to decide where AI helps and where a predictable interface is better.

Make the system understandable

Show what the system is doing, make generated results editable and keep a clear route back. Confidence comes from understandable behaviour, not an animated loading state.

Keep people in control

Use human review where errors would matter. Give people a chance to check details, change a suggestion and decide when an action happens.

A practical AI workflow for creative teams

Creative practice · Studio note

AI can help a creative team explore more directions, but a useful process still needs a brief, a point of view and an editor. Treat generated material as a starting point for judgement.

Write the brief first

Set out the audience, the message, the constraints and the decision you need to make. Use those questions to evaluate suggestions instead of collecting variations without a purpose.

Separate exploration from production

Use exploratory work to learn what a direction could be. Before production, check rights, source files, factual claims and how the assets will behave in the final medium.

Document the decisions

Keep a short record of what was selected and why. Naming, versioning and reusable guidelines make the next round of work more consistent.

Why simpler websites create more value

Web design · Studio note

Simplicity is the result of clear priorities. A useful website gives its audience enough information to decide what to do next, without making every section compete for attention.

Build a clear reading order

Give each section one job. Use a descriptive heading, useful supporting copy and a next step that follows naturally from the content.

Design the small screens first

Check the main tasks on a narrow viewport. Readable text, comfortable controls and purposeful content are more valuable than compressing a desktop layout.

Measure what matters

Start with observable tasks: finding a service, understanding an offer or completing an enquiry. Use feedback to improve those journeys rather than adding decorative complexity.

A clearer brief. A better start.

A useful brief does not have to be long. These questions help turn an idea into a shared starting point.

  • The goal: What needs to change, and why now?
  • The audience: Who is this for, and what do they need to do?
  • The scope: What needs to be designed or built?
  • The context: What brand assets, content or technology already exist?
  • The constraints: What timing, budget or review requirements matter?
  • The outcome: How will you judge whether the work is useful?
